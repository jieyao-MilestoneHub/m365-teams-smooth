# Meeting Follow-through Policy

## Purpose and scope
This policy governs what happens to commitments spoken in meetings — standups, project syncs, design
reviews, and ad-hoc discussions. It exists because action items agreed verbally or in chat are the
most commonly lost unit of work: they have no owner of record, no due date, and no system tracking
them. It applies to every recurring meeting and to any discussion in which a participant commits to
deliver something.

## Action items must be tracked, not remembered
Every action item agreed in a meeting must be recorded in the team's task tracker with three fields:
a single named owner, a concrete due date, and a title that states the deliverable. An action item
that lives only in the meeting discussion, a chat thread, or a participant's memory is not considered
assigned. Follow-through accountability belongs to the owner of record, and the tracker — not the
chat history — is the system of record for who owes what.

## Owners and due dates are mandatory
An action item without a named owner is unowned and must either be assigned in the meeting or
explicitly dropped. An action item without a due date is not schedulable; when the discussion names a
date, that date is binding, and when it does not, the owner must set one when the task is created.
Shared ownership ("the team will…") is recorded against the meeting's facilitator until reassigned.

## Proposals without dates become scheduled events
When a meeting proposes a follow-up activity without a date — "let's review progress next week" — the
proposal must be converted into a scheduled calendar event rather than left as an intention. A
proposal that is neither scheduled nor tracked is treated as dropped.

## Review cadence
Open action items are reviewed at the start of the next recurring meeting. Items past their due date
are either re-dated with the owner's commitment or escalated; silently carrying overdue items forward
is not permitted.
