# Status Reporting Policy

## Purpose and scope
This policy governs recurring project status reports — what they contain, where they are published,
and what sources they draw from. It exists because status information is scattered across systems
(the issue tracker, the task planner, calendars, and chat), and ad-hoc reporting wastes the time of
both the author, who re-collects the same facts weekly, and the audience, who cannot trust which
version is current. It applies to every active project with more than one stakeholder.

## Weekly cadence, single source
Each active project publishes one status report per week on a fixed day. The report is posted to the
project's designated channel and that post is the single source of record for the week; side-channel
summaries, private recaps, and per-stakeholder variants are discouraged because they fork the record.
A report that is not posted to the channel of record is not considered published.

## Reports aggregate the systems of record
A status report draws from the systems where the work actually lives: issues closed in the source
repository, tasks completed or tracked in the planner, and meetings held on the calendar. The report
summarizes counts and highlights; the underlying systems remain the authoritative detail. Numbers in
a report must be reproducible from those sources — a report is an aggregation, never a separate
ledger.

## Blockers are named
A status report that omits known blockers is incomplete. Each open blocker is named with its owner
and the decision or dependency it waits on, so the report is actionable rather than ceremonial.

## Reports are records
Published reports are retained as part of the project record and are not edited after publication;
corrections are posted as follow-ups. The reporting history is the project's timeline of record for
audits and retrospectives.
