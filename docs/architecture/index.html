AI Change Court — Architecture

A risky enterprise decision goes on trial before it becomes action: gather impact evidence, draft a feasible plan — or a safer alternative — resolve who must approve, collect the verdict, execute, and keep an audit nobody can rewrite. Six views, each a self-contained offline page.

How to read these six views

They are ordered from the outside in, then from flow to data: start with the container layers (①), follow a decision through the court pipeline (②) and its human approval gate (③), then see what protects the path (④), what persists (⑤), and where the Microsoft platform plugs in (⑥). Every page carries its own diagram, design rationale, guarantees, and links — read one in isolation or all six in order.

  1. ①Layered container architecture

    Dependency-inversion layers from the M365 entry through the FastAPI façades to the pure domain — why the core never touches an SDK, and why a new integration is one adapter plus one registration.

  2. ②Court pipeline & governed autonomy

    The LangGraph court with its courtroom roles, the agentic-vs-deterministic split, the durable verdict interrupt, and the precedent memory loop.

  3. ③Approval sequence & separation of duties

    Submit → send → decide → execute as a time-ordered sequence — the requester ≠ approver gate, derived quorum, and the idempotent verdict gate.

  4. ④Security & trust boundaries

    Four defense lines from the OAuth2 edge to adapter parameter validation, plus the append-only audit and the keyless credential posture.

  5. ⑤Data model & retention

    The permanent audit as source of truth, purgeable working storage, and rebuildable read models — including the court's explainable precedent memory.

  6. ⑥Microsoft platform integration

    Where Microsoft 365 Copilot, Azure AI Foundry (Azure OpenAI + Foundry IQ), Microsoft Graph, and GitHub plug in — and which edges are keyless, credentialed, or mocked.
